iSpeak Confidence V16.0.7 — IELTS Professional Rebuild QA Audit
================================================================

LEGAL / SOURCE APPROACH
- Official IELTS.org resources are linked externally for learners.
- Official IELTS questions, passages, recordings, graphics and model material are NOT copied into the app.
- iSpeak Confidence IELTS practice passages, scripts, questions and prompts are original practice content.
- The IELTS page states that iSpeak Confidence is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the IELTS Partners.
- Before a commercial public launch, trademark/branding usage should still receive appropriate legal review.

OFFICIAL ALIGNMENT USED
- Listening: 4 parts, 10 questions per part, 40 total.
- Reading: 40 questions, 60-minute format.
- Academic Reading full mocks: about 2,650 words each across 3 passages.
- Writing: Task 1 minimum 150 words / about 20 minutes; Task 2 minimum 250 words / about 40 minutes.
- Writing feedback uses the four published IELTS criteria; Task 2 is weighted more heavily.
- Speaking: 3 parts, 11–14 minutes; Part 2 includes preparation and an extended response.
- Academic and General Training Reading/Writing use separate mock material.

IELTS CONTENT REBUILD
- 6 progressive IELTS levels.
- 72 total IELTS study units.
- Each level now has distinct topics rather than recycling the same 12 topics under a new band label.
- Difficulty increases from test literacy/foundation skills through inference, synthesis, nuance and advanced argument control.
- 6 full mock tests: 3 Academic + 3 General Training.
- Every mock has 40 Listening and 40 Reading questions.
- Listening mock scripts are substantially longer and use distinct social / orientation / academic discussion / lecture contexts.
- Reading mocks are full-length practice sets rather than short mini-passages.
- Listening and Reading mock questions are now answerable and scoreable inside the app.
- Practice band guides are explicitly labelled approximate, not official results.
- Device text-to-speech is labelled as practice simulation audio, not official/native IELTS recording.

WHAT IS STILL SHALLOW OUTSIDE IELTS
The main seven-language curriculum still needs a separate expansion before it can honestly be marketed as a full beginner-to-high-level language course:
- Khmer: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- English: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- Mandarin: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- Spanish: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- French: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- Japanese: 60 unique target anchors across 30 units (2 per unit).
- Arabic: 120 unique target anchors across 30 units (4 per unit).

The last 10 units do contain harder free-production/conversation challenges, but the amount of explicitly taught new target language is still too small to justify calling the complete language courses B2/C1 mastery pathways.

IMPORTANT KHMER CONSTRAINT
No new Khmer learner-facing language was invented in this rebuild. Khmer expansion should continue only from user-approved Khmer source material.

OTHER REMAINING PUBLISHING ITEMS
- AI features remain inactive until the Gemini environment variables are configured.
- Email remains inactive until SMTP environment variables are configured.
- Production user accounts should use persistent DATA_DIR storage / persistent disk or a database.
- Human-recorded multi-speaker IELTS mock audio would still be a quality upgrade over browser TTS.
- A final trademark/branding review is advisable before a commercial public launch.

CONCLUSION
The IELTS section is no longer the main shallow/repetition problem. Its structure, progression, mock length, question counts and content variety are now substantially closer to a professional preparation product. The next major content bottleneck is the seven-language curriculum depth, not IELTS.
